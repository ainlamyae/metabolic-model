\section{System Diagram}

\subsection{Human Metabolic System Diagram}

The human metabolic system is a multi-input, single-output system: several inputs act simultaneously on a single output, the measured body mass m. Height, age, and sex (h, a, σ) are fixed parameters, whereas activity (τ, MET), total energy intake (TEI), and sleep duration (s) are controllable inputs, each entering the Energy Balance through its own path. Driving m toward the healthy body mass m_des therefore requires regulating all controllable inputs together; an input left uncontrolled acts as an unregulated disturbance on D that the remaining inputs cannot reliably compensate for, and the output drifts from its desire.

\subsection{Inverse Metabolic System as Feedforward}

The inverse model runs the human metabolic system of Fig.~1 backwards, as shown in Fig.~2. Like the forward model, it reads the measured body mass m through the 7-day rolling average m̄, height, age, and sex (h, a, σ) through the basal metabolic rate, and the elapsed time t; to these it adds the desired body mass change Δm\_des, which defines the daily energy deficit D = Δm\_des×ρ/7. The energy balance then splits the intake that delivers D into its components, so the remaining inputs of the forward model become outputs of the inverse: activity (τ, MET), total energy intake (TEI), and sleep duration (s), with TEI in turn setting the daily protein, dietary fiber, fat, and carbohydrate requirements of Section 5. The inverse model assumes the glycogen and water swing ΔM\_gly is constant, so the whole desired change is taken as fat mass and the Glycogen + Water Swing block is left out.

\subsection{Full-Loop Human Metabolic Control}

The full loop places a human decision maker between the two models, as shown in Fig.~3. The inverse model proposes the daily inputs — activity, intake, sleep, and the macronutrient requirements — as a feedforward, but it acts on nothing itself: the human brain weighs that proposal and decides what is actually eaten, how much is moved, and how long is slept. Those actual inputs drive the human metabolic system, the system under control, and its measured body mass returns to the inverse model, which proposes again; any gap between the proposed and the actual inputs reaches the body as a disturbance that the next proposal corrects.
